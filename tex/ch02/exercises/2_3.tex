\begin{itemize}
    \item[(a)] An annual rate of 3\% compounded monthly will produce an
        increase of ${(1 + r)}^n = {(1.0025)}^{12}$, which we approximate by the
        following
        \begin{lstlisting}
>>> from investment_science.cash_flows import growth_factor
>>> growth_factor(0.03 / 12, 12)
1.0304159569135067
        \end{lstlisting}
        Thus the effective rate is given by 3.04\%.
    \item[(b)] An annual rate of 18\% compounded monthly will produce an
        increase of ${(1.015)}^{12}$, which we approximate by the following
        \begin{lstlisting}
>>> growth_factor(0.18 / 12, 12)
1.195618171461534
        \end{lstlisting}
        Thus the effective rate is given by 19.56\%.
    \item[(c)] An annual rate of 18\% compounded quarterly will produce an
        increase of ${(1.045)}^4$, which we approximate by the following
        \begin{lstlisting}
>>> growth_factor(0.18 / 4, 4)
1.1925186006249997
        \end{lstlisting}
        Thus the effective rate is given by 19.25\%.
\end{itemize}
